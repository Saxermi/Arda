# Advanced sparse and operator solvers

**Status** — Research candidates and follow-ups for Aulë and distributed execution. The implemented
solver, preconditioner, verification and Poisson contracts are in [Aulë numerical solvers](../implemented/aule-solvers.md).
The historical baselines are [Moria CG/PCG](../../docs/MORIA_0.14.3.md#solvers-and-budget) and [the 0.13 CGLS implementation](../../docs/legacy-reference-de.md#module-high-native-c).
Let $n$ be the dimension, $z$ stored entries, and $k$ the restart or block basis size.

| Capability | Candidate and alternative | Cost and numerical trade-off | Implementation and validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Symmetric, nonsymmetric and rectangular systems | Use CG/PCG for SPD, MINRES for symmetric indefinite systems, restarted GMRES for nonsymmetric systems, and LSQR for rectangular least squares. Compare LSMR and BiCGSTAB where normal-residual behavior or workspace warrants. | CSR applies cost $O(n+z)$ for square matrices. CG, MINRES, BiCGSTAB and LSQR keep a fixed number of vectors. GMRES stores $O(nk)$ and uses up to $O(nk^2)$ orthogonalization work per cycle plus applies. Restart can stagnate. BiCGSTAB may break down or show irregular residuals. MINRES preconditioning must preserve the required inner product. | Moderate CPU effort if the operator exposes apply, transpose and workspace. Preserve true residual, convergence reason and meaningful data/normal-residual metrics. Test singular, inconsistent, indefinite and nonnormal cases against SciPy or PETSc, and test capped storage. |
| Preconditioners and sparse factorization | Start with Jacobi/block Jacobi, then compare incomplete Cholesky for SPD and ILU(0) for general systems. Consider supernodal sparse LU as an optional provider for repeated direct solves. | Local triangular preconditioners trade setup cost and synchronization for fewer iterations. ILU and incomplete Cholesky can encounter bad pivots. Sparse LU work and space depend on ordering and fill and can approach dense costs. | Screen fill and workspace before setup. Add diagonally weak, badly ordered and anisotropic cases. Compare setup amortization and repeated solves. High effort for portable sparse LU. |
| Multigrid and iterative refinement | Start with geometric multigrid on known grids and add explicit mixed-precision refinement only with a high-precision residual and fallback. Compare optional AMG, smoothers and block methods. | A V-cycle is $O(N)$ under bounded work per site and regular coarsening. Convergence depends on smoothing and coarse correction, especially on anisotropic grids. Refinement must satisfy conditioning and precision limits. | Moderate effort for a small grid family, high for general AMG. Use the 128³ manufactured-solution gate, mesh refinement, difficult coefficients, residual and normalized solution error, setup, RSS and time. Test overflow and fallback when low precision fails. |
| Partial spectra and verification | Lanczos for symmetric operators and Arnoldi for general ones, with reorthogonalization as necessary. Compute scaled backward error and an inexpensive condition estimate in addition to residual. | Each expansion uses an apply and up to $O(nk)$ reorthogonalization, with $O(nk)$ basis storage. Loss of orthogonality creates false clusters. Small residuals may coexist with large forward error on sensitive eigenpairs. | Treat subspace overlap and eigenpair residuals as distinct checks. Test clustered eigenvalues and early breakdown with independent dense references. Keep verification in the result contract and avoid unconditional convergence claims. |

**Research evidence** — Compare classic Krylov recurrences and direct sparse methods first. Mixed-precision augmented GMRES and normal-equation preconditioning are recent candidates where restart and finite-precision behavior are limiting. Pipelined CG can hide global reductions but may reduce attainable accuracy, so retain classic PCG as the numerical baseline and test it against communication-avoiding variants.
