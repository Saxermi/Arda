# Numerical libraries

**Status** — Candidates beyond the initial 0.15 releases. The implemented notes cover [reusable factors](../implemented/linear-algebra.md#reusable-float64-factorizations) and the [scalar mathematical library](../implemented/mathematical-library.md). Cost models below describe standard serial algorithms on CPU, with shared matrix data and output storage excluded unless stated.

| Capability | Baseline and alternatives | Cost, reliability and constraints | Implementation and local validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alternative SVD and eigenvalue methods | [SVD](../implemented/linear-algebra.md#lapack-reduced-svd) and [eigensolvers](../implemented/linear-algebra.md#lapack-eigensolvers) use optional LAPACK. [Randomized SVD](../implemented/structured-computing.md#randomized-svd) uses subspace iteration. Compare divide and conquer, preconditioned Jacobi, and randomized block Krylov. | Block Krylov retains a larger basis for the same sketch block width. Measure its accuracy, workspace and number of matrix products on matched spectra. Eigenvector conditioning needs care. | Compare residuals and subspaces against the implemented baselines. Validate portable and approximate methods on their documented domains. |
| Parallel random generation and extended statistics | Build on the [serial random and statistics modules](../implemented/random-and-statistics.md). Compare counter-based streams for parallel workloads, selection for exact quantiles, and sketches for explicit approximations. | Pairwise-mergeable moments add parallel reductions. Full covariance of $d$ features takes $O(d^2)$ state. Reduction order can affect reproducibility. | Verify seeded stream semantics, merge order, tail quantiles, degenerate cases, and large-offset variance. Compare performance with the existing serial contracts at matched accuracy. |
| Special functions, roots and interpolation | Compare platform libm and documented piecewise rational/asymptotic methods for Gamma, Beta, Bessel and erf. Use bracketed Brent for roots, safeguarded Newton where derivatives are known, and piecewise cubic splines or shape-preserving interpolation by contract. | Fixed rational evaluations have bounded work per region but need difficult coefficient and domain validation. Root methods require a finite bracket or a separate local-convergence contract. A natural cubic spline has $O(n)$ tridiagonal setup and $O(\log n)$ indexed evaluation. | Start with argument conventions and well-tested provider calls. Use independent high precision across poles, tails, underflow and transitions. Validate spline boundary conditions and monotonically constrained cases. Cost is high for a full portable special-function suite. |
| Adaptive quadrature and convex optimization | Use adaptive Gauss–Kronrod with subdivision and an error estimate. Keep Simpson as a smooth-grid baseline and compare specialized transforms for endpoint singularities. Evaluate proximal gradient for smooth-plus-prox objectives before general constrained methods. Retain L-BFGS for compatible smooth optimization. | Adaptive integration uses $O(E)$ evaluations for $E$ samples and bounded interval storage, with no uniform guarantee for unresolved singularities. Proximal gradient costs one gradient and prox evaluation per iteration and has conditional convergence under convexity and step assumptions. | Validate errors and work limits against analytical integrals and SciPy at matched tolerances. Check explicit failure on hard integrands. For optimization, test KKT or proximal residuals, scaling and nonsmooth examples. A generic constrained solver has higher implementation cost than a narrow convex path. |

**Research evidence** — Halko, Martinsson and Tropp provide a baseline for randomized decompositions. The 2026 block-Krylov paper targets low-rank approximations of *matrix functions*. It motivates a separate operator-based experiment rather than a replacement for SVD. The 2026 hybrid Simpson/Gaussian study is a comparator for adaptive quadrature. Establish Gauss–Kronrod and independent error checks first. [Halko et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1137/090771806) · [Block Krylov 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2026.03.029) · [Hybrid quadrature 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335582)
